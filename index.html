Chapter I

The Problem We Solve

If you run a university endowment with a 5% spending rule, you face a cruel arithmetic. A single year of −30% equity returns doesn't just hurt—it erases six years of spending capacity. The standard answer has been "60/40." But when equity-bond correlations flipped positive in 2022, that answer stopped working.

Our clients need something different: returns that don't care whether the market goes up or down. That's not a marketing slogan—it's a mathematical constraint. Beta must be zero. Alpha must be positive. Everything we do flows from those two requirements.

Chapter II

How We Think About Markets

We don't predict where markets are going. We identify themes where the market has priced two related assets differently, and we buy the cheap one and sell the expensive one. The market can go wherever it wants—we profit from the gap closing.

We call these "thematic baskets." Each basket is designed to be market-neutral on its own. If European defense spending is rising, we don't just buy defense stocks—we buy European defense ETFs and short Airbus, capturing the rotation without the direction.

Chapter IV

The Discipline

A market-neutral fund without discipline is just a leveraged bet with extra steps. Our Investment Committee meets weekly. Every position requires majority approval. We auto-rebalance when any position moves ±20%. We keep each basket's net exposure within 1% of target.

The hardest rule to follow: when a pair moves against you, the instinct is to wait. Our process says rebalance. This saved us twice.

Chapter VI

Where It Hurt

The short book cost us $35,000. That's the honest number. TSLA, HPQ, and BBWI—three shorts that moved against us during a sharp risk-on rally in April. The long book made $12,000. Net: we're up, but barely.

The lesson: pairs trading in a momentum-driven market requires tighter rebalancing than we initially planned. We adjusted—moving from weekly to real-time threshold monitoring. Our Alpha Dashboard now flags any position that drifts beyond targets.

Chapter VII

How We Measure Risk

Value at Risk tells you the loss you shouldn't exceed on 95% of days. Ours is $5,500—about half a percent. But VaR is a fair-weather metric. What we really want to know is: what happens when correlations spike?

We stress-tested against five historical crises. The worst case—a GFC-level event with correlations inflated 50%—would cost us an estimated 6.7%. Painful, but survivable. A long-only portfolio would lose 56%.

Estimated portfolio loss under historical crisis scenarios

We also monitor risks that don't show up in a VaR calculation: headline risk from geopolitical events that move our defense positions overnight, gap risk from earnings surprises in illiquid names, and personnel risk—we're four people, and if one leaves, the process breaks.

Chapter VIII

The Infrastructure

We built something unusual for a class project: a real-time portfolio analytics platform. The Alpha Dashboard ingests our StockTrak positions, runs factor models, computes VaR, and monitors exposure—all automatically.

This isn't a nice-to-have. A market-neutral fund that checks its beta once a week is flying blind. Our dashboard shows beta-adjusted net exposure in real time. When it drifts past 10%, we rebalance.

Chapter IX

What We'd Do Differently

Six weeks is not enough time. With six months, we would have built a statistically meaningful track record. With a year, we could test whether our alpha survives transaction costs at scale.

Three specific improvements for the next quarter:

  1. Options overlay for tail risk protection—buying cheap puts on correlated indices to hedge the scenarios VaR doesn't capture.
  2. Automated rebalancing triggers—currently manual, should fire automatically when any position breaches drift thresholds.
  3. Factor-based signal generation to complement fundamental research—using momentum, value, and quality factors to time pair entries.
Chapter X

The Bottom Line

Threshing Hedge demonstrated that a disciplined, technology-enabled market-neutral process can generate positive returns independent of market direction. Our beta is near zero. Our Sharpe is 1.93. Our process is systematic and documented.

We don't claim to have proven alpha in 22 trading days. What we've proven is that the process works—that it's possible to build a genuine market-neutral portfolio across five asset classes with a four-person team, a StockTrak account, and a clear investment philosophy.
